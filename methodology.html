Methodology

Fixed rules, published in advance. Nothing here implies guaranteed profit.

What is measured

Every result is in R: profit or loss divided by the initial risk per unit, Ru = |fill − initial stop|. Results are net of modeled fees, spread, slippage and financing (carry), computed from published prices. A win means a result above 0R; results within ±0.1R are also shown as scratches. Void and expired signals count as 0R and are listed and counted separately.

At 1% account risk per trade, 1R ≈ 1% of the account (hypothetical, simple sum, before taxes).

Entry and fills

Signals are evaluated on the closed daily bar. The recorded fill is the open of the first tradable hourly bar at or after the next whole hour following publication — never earlier than a reader could act. Each signal carries a valid entry range and a valid-until time. On the adverse side the range ends 0.5 × ATR(20) beyond the reference price; on the favorable side it ends halfway between the reference price and the stop. A fill through the stop is VOID (gap through stop); otherwise a fill outside the range is VOID (gap beyond range); no tradable price in the window is EXPIRED.

Entry rules

  • Time-series momentum (indices, FX, commodities, ETFs; crypto long only): 1-, 3- and 12-month returns all agree.
  • Donchian breakout (Turtle System 2): close beyond the 55-day high or low.
  • 52-week high (US stocks, long only): new 52-week closing high above the 200-day average. Qualified on forward results only.
  • Crypto breakout ensemble (long only): breakout confirmed on several lookbacks between 20 and 250 days.
  • Index mean reversion (long only): short pullback (RSI-2) inside a long-term uptrend.

Evidence labels refer to the entry rule only. The published research behind them studied related but not identical systems (often continuous, volatility-scaled positions). The exit plan below is Blip's own and is measured against a pure trailing-stop baseline (“plan − baseline” below).

Exits

Trend rules: a resting initial stop; one third closed at TP1, after which the stop moves to the entry price; one third at TP2; the last third trails a chandelier stop that never loosens. Every stop change and scheduled exit is announced first and becomes effective at least one hour after it is posted. Mean reversion: a disaster stop, an exit at the next open after a close above the 5-day average, and a time stop.

Qualification and monitoring

A strategy family (rule × asset class) publishes only after passing a pre-set statistical gate on out-of-sample backtests and a forward shadow period. Backtests are hypothetical. Parameters are fixed per strategy version and never re-optimised. Live results are monitored against limits set in advance; crossing one pauses the family (open trades keep being reported). A portfolio drawdown of 40R pauses all public signals.

Known biases and caveats

  • Only post-launch forward results are genuinely out-of-sample. Backtest and live results are never mixed.
  • The live record uses data as published at signal time; backtests use revised data.
  • FX backtests fill at the next daily open (about one hour earlier than live): “approximate fill”.
  • For instruments priced from free vendor data, live tracking on hourly bars can differ from daily tracking; the measured tracking divergence (mean and 95% CI) is shown above.
  • Crypto backtests use a point-in-time universe including delisted pairs; ETF and FX selection may carry a small residual survivorship bias.
  • ETF prices differ from futures and CFDs; levels are also given as % distances.

How to verify

Every signal (public and lab) and every lifecycle event is appended to a hash-chained log. Open trades are committed to the log as salted hashes and revealed in full when they close. In results-only mode nothing actionable is public while a trade is open; in live mode public signals are posted in real time. Each day's log head is timestamped with OpenTimestamps (heads/), and this site's git history is a second, independent timestamp trail.

python verify_ledger.py ledger.jsonl

The script uses the Python standard library only. It checks the chain and that every reveal matches its earlier commitments. This is a hash-chained, timestamped log.
